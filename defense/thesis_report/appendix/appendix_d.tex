\chapter{Significance test details}
\label{app:significance}

This appendix gives the procedure behind the paired comparisons of \Cref{sec:significance}: how CAEM's and each baseline's answers are paired, the exact-match and hallucination tests, the bootstrap intervals, the Holm correction and the rule that turns a test into a win or a defeat. Every number below is read from the final test record, which is rebuilt by the results command of \Cref{app:reproducibility}.

\section{Pairing rule and correctness label}
\label{app:sig-pairing}

\textbf{Pairing:} each contrast compares CAEM at one cycle (C0, C2 or C5) with one baseline on one benchmark. The answers of the two systems are matched on the question identifier, restricted to the evaluation-fold identifiers of the dataset-split record, so a contrast uses only questions both systems answered. Under the comparison protocol of \Cref{sec:setup-protocol} every system answered the same $300$ questions per benchmark, and every contrast in this appendix has $n = 300$ pairs.

\textbf{Correctness label:} both sides are scored with the single rule of \Cref{sec:setup-metrics}: the uniform scorer on FEVER, TriviaQA, CommonsenseQA and StrategyQA, and on TruthfulQA the language-model judge applied to the final answer line, the same judge for every system. The earlier ROUGE-based TruthfulQA field is not read.

\textbf{Hallucination value:} the per-question CHM is the share of the seven active failure flags a question raises (\Cref{sec:setup-metrics}). Each baseline answer carries the verifier signals of the comparison protocol, so B8 is tested on this axis as well, and no baseline is charged with a decision it did not take.

\section{Contingency tables}
\label{app:sig-tables}

For exact match, each contrast forms the paired two-by-two table of correct and wrong answers. The two off-diagonal cells are the discordant pairs: $b$ counts questions CAEM answers correctly and the baseline does not, and $c$ the reverse. The McNemar test reads only these two cells, through the continuity-corrected statistic of Edwards~\cite{edwards1948note} with one degree of freedom,
\[
\chi^{2} \;=\; \frac{\bigl(\lvert b - c \rvert - 1\bigr)^{2}}{b + c},
\]
with $p = 1$ when $b + c = 0$. \Cref{tab:app-sig-tables} shows four contrasts at C5: a tie with the zero-shot baseline, CAEM's largest win and its largest defeat, both against vanilla fine-tuning, and a defeat by five-shot chain of thought.

For the composite hallucination metric, the per-question differences (CAEM minus baseline) are tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}, two-sided, with questions of zero difference dropped.

\section{Bootstrap protocol}
\label{app:sig-bootstrap}

The interval on each difference is the percentile bootstrap over paired questions: $2{,}000$ resamples of the $300$ pairs with replacement, from a generator seeded with $42$, with the $95$ percent interval read from the $2.5$ and $97.5$ percentiles of the resampled means. The same resampling gives the interval on the CHM difference. The percentile method is used rather than the bias-corrected and accelerated method because the per-question exact-match difference takes only the values $-1$, $0$ and $+1$, on which the acceleration estimate is unstable at this sample size.

The interval describes the size of a difference; whether a contrast counts as significant is decided by the Holm-corrected test of the next section. \Cref{tab:app-sig-boot-intervals} shows why the two are reported separately: the interval of the retrieval baseline on TriviaQA excludes zero, but the contrast does not survive the correction for eight comparisons.

\section{Holm sequence}
\label{app:sig-holm}

The Holm--Bonferroni correction~\cite{holm1979simple} is applied within each benchmark, separately on each axis, over the $m = 8$ baselines. The raw $p$-values are sorted in ascending order, the $k$-th smallest is multiplied by $m - k + 1$, a running maximum keeps the adjusted values in order, and values above one are set to one. The family-wise error rate is held at $0.05$ within each benchmark. \Cref{tab:app-sig-holm-tqa,tab:app-sig-holm-fever} give the exact-match sequences at C5 for TriviaQA, where CAEM's two wins lie, and for CommonsenseQA, the one benchmark with both wins and defeats.

\section{Win and defeat rule}
\label{app:sig-licensing}

A contrast counts as a win or a defeat only when two conditions hold together: the Holm-corrected $p$-value is below $0.05$, and the absolute difference is at least $0.02$ in exact match or $0.01$ in CHM. The direction of the difference decides whether it is a CAEM win or a baseline win (for CHM, lower is better). The second condition matters because with $300$ paired questions a test can detect differences too small to matter; the floors keep such contrasts out of the counts. The floors are the same for every contrast, cycle and baseline.

\textbf{Counts:} over the $40$ contrasts per cycle ($8$ baselines on $5$ benchmarks), exact match gives $9$ CAEM wins and $2$ defeats at C0, $9$ and $3$ at C2, and $5$ and $3$ at C5; CHM gives $6$ wins and $17$ defeats at C0, $13$ and $5$ at C2, and $10$ and $4$ at C5 (\Cref{tab:sig-summary}). Against the prompting baselines and semantic-entropy abstention (B1, B2, B5, B8), no exact-match contrast is a CAEM win at any cycle. The exact-match defeats at C2 and C5 are against vanilla fine-tuning on FEVER and CommonsenseQA and five-shot chain of thought on CommonsenseQA.

\textbf{Verifier-free check:} the same tests on the CHM restricted to the five subtypes that use neither CAEM's composite score nor its decisions give $15$ wins and $4$ defeats at C0, $28$ and $2$ at C2, and $31$ and $3$ at C5. CAEM fares better on this measure than on the full CHM, partly by construction, as discussed in \Cref{sec:disc-threat-verifier}.

\chapter*{Abstract}
Large Language Models produce fluent but factually incorrect responses at rates reported up to 40\% in open-domain question answering, and higher in specialised domains. This problem limits their usage in high-stakes settings. Current models cannot improve on their own because they operate statelessly, without any verified knowledge accumulation across interactions. This research proposes CAEM (Confidence-Aware Episodic Memory with Self-Improvement), a system that progressively reduces hallucinations through verified episodic storage and constrained iterative fine-tuning on the verified episodes. CAEM integrates three core mechanisms. First, a verified episodic memory populated only through a four-outcome storage decision (\textsc{Store}, \textsc{Deferred}, \textsc{Abstain}, \textsc{Discard}) driven by a nine-signal post-generation verifier organised in three complementary families: internal calibration (token probability, MC-dropout variance, internal-state probe), sample-set agreement (average pairwise chain similarity, chain-to-answer entailment, normalised semantic entropy), and external grounding (weakest-link and mean atomic-fact entailment, ensemble contradiction probability). A bounded FIFO buffer holds \textsc{Deferred} entries for reconsideration at the next cycle boundary. Second, a confidence-aware three-tier router that combines memory-match similarity with stored verifier confidence into a single dispatch score, and an independent safety override on a pre-routing confidence signal that forces retrieval-augmented generation whenever pre-routing confidence falls below a fixed floor, regardless of memory-match quality. Third, a constrained self-improvement loop that, at each cycle boundary, retroactively re-verifies every stored episode under the updated model (upward-only updates), reconsiders deferred entries, and fine-tunes under an $L_2$ anchor toward the base-model parameters as an EWC approximation; a hard out-of-distribution retention guard on MMLU rolls back the weights (but not the memory) whenever general capability erodes beyond a fixed floor. The system targets a substantial reduction in confident-error rate, defined as the fraction of stored, high-confidence responses that are incorrect, measured against a zero-shot Flan-T5-Large baseline on a seven-benchmark factual-QA panel (FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, HaluEval, TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, ARC-Challenge) with MMLU as the out-of-distribution retention control; the specific quantitative reduction is reported in the results chapter. The study also argues, under a data-purity analysis, that even an imperfect verifier can drive monotonic improvement across cycles when the training-pool purity threshold is set above the storage threshold.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\Cref{sec:exp-setup} defines the evaluation protocol used across the
remainder of this chapter: the six benchmarks \caem{} is evaluated on,
the metric suite applied to each benchmark at each cycle, and the eight
external baselines against which \caem{} is compared (B1--B8), plus an
optional STaR ceiling reference reported when compute permits. The
metric suite is deliberately broader than prior single-number
evaluations of hallucination systems: a single exact-match score cannot
disentangle accuracy from calibration, grounding, confabulation, or
forgetting, and a thesis claiming an \emph{architectural} intervention
must demonstrate behaviour on each of these axes separately before any
aggregate claim is accepted.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\caem{} is evaluated on six factual-QA benchmarks spanning three
in-domain training benchmarks (FEVER~\cite{thorne-etal-2018-fever},
TriviaQA~\cite{joshi-etal-2017-triviaqa}, and Natural
Questions~\cite{kwiatkowski-etal-2019-natural}) and three
out-of-domain transfer benchmarks
(TruthfulQA~\cite{lin-etal-2022-truthfulqa},
StrategyQA~\cite{10.1162/tacl_a_00370},
and ARC-Challenge~\cite{clark2018think}),
with MMLU~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring} held out as the out-of-distribution
retention control for the forgetting-tolerance guard.
The confident-error axis of \caem{}'s claim is evaluated via the
three-sub-type hallucination decomposition
(\emph{confabulation}, \emph{ungrounded}, \emph{contradiction})
defined in~\cref{sec:metrics} and reported in~\cref{tab:halluc},
rather than through a dedicated external hallucination-annotated
benchmark; this keeps the benchmark panel focused on factual
correctness while still measuring the confident-error rate directly
against the stored $(\ustored, \mathrm{correct})$ stream.
This split cleanly separates the in-domain contribution of each
architectural mechanism from its zero-shot transfer effect; the
ablation study in~\cref{sec:ablation} exploits this separation.
Benchmark selection rationale and dataset statistics are given in
\cref{tab:benchmark-overview}.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Every cycle of every benchmark produces a per-sample record containing
the generated answer, the reasoning chain, the routing tier, wall-clock
latency, and the full set of verification signals emitted by the
\textsc{UnifiedVerifier} at Stage~5. The metrics below are computed
from these per-sample records and are reported in seven dedicated
tables (Tables~\ref{tab:headline}--\ref{tab:sig-test}). We organise the
suite into seven categories, each answering a distinct evaluation
question.

\paragraph{Accuracy and latency (Table~\ref{tab:headline}).}
Primary correctness is reported with exact match (EM) and token-level
F1 on free-form answers (TriviaQA, NQ), label accuracy on classification
benchmarks (FEVER three-way, ARC-Challenge four-way,
StrategyQA binary), and ROUGE-L$\geq 0.15$ on TruthfulQA following
Lin et al.~\cite{lin-etal-2022-truthfulqa}.
Because \caem{}'s architectural value lies in its routing selectivity,
each benchmark's EM is also reported
\emph{stratified by routing tier} -- $\mathrm{EM}_{T_1},
\mathrm{EM}_{T_2}, \mathrm{EM}_{T_3}$ -- so that a rise in Tier~1
fraction (memory hits) across cycles can be read alongside per-tier
correctness rather than being averaged away.
Wall-clock latency is reported per tier for the same reason: Tier~1
skips full decoding, so the efficiency claim reduces to ``Tier~1
latency is substantially below Tier~2 and Tier~3 latency, and Tier~1
share grows across cycles.'' Both quantities are computed via
\texttt{em\_by\_tier} and \texttt{mean\_latency\_by\_tier} in
\texttt{eval/metrics.py}.

\paragraph{Calibration (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}).}
Calibration is quantified with three complementary metrics computed on
the pooled stream of $(\ustored, \mathrm{correct})$ pairs from every
stored episode across all six benchmarks at each cycle.
The \emph{Expected Calibration Error} (ECE; Guo et
al.~\cite{pmlr-v70-guo17a}) measures average absolute gap between
confidence and accuracy over $M=10$ equal-width bins.
The Brier score~\cite{brier1950verification}
complements ECE: it is a proper scoring rule that penalises both
miscalibration and low resolution, and it does not require bin
aggregation -- a system that is badly calibrated in ways that
counter-balance inside bins will still receive a poor Brier score
even as ECE remains low.
The \emph{area under the ROC curve} (AUROC) treats $\ustored$ as a
predictor of correctness and measures rank discrimination independent
of scale; a high AUROC with poor ECE indicates that the confidence
signal is useful for routing even before calibration.
Bin-wise \emph{reliability diagrams} (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability})
ground these scalars visually using the 10-bin decomposition returned
by \texttt{reliability\_bins}.

\paragraph{Hallucination decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:halluc}).}
A single hallucination-rate scalar conflates distinct failure modes,
so we report the \emph{pooled confident-error rate} (the fraction
of stored episodes with $\ustored \geq 0.50$ and $\mathrm{EM}=0$,
which is the quantity the data purity theorem bounds and the quantity
the pre-registered $\geq 40\%$ reduction target in
\cref{sec:evaluation} is evaluated against) alongside three
diagnostic sub-type rates named after the failure modes defined in
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} and targeted by the three verifier
families.
The \emph{confabulation rate} is the fraction of samples that are
both wrong ($\mathrm{EM}=0$) and semantically unstable
($h_{\text{norm}} \geq 0.50$ over the $K=10$ answer cluster),
following the semantic-entropy notion of Farquhar et
al.~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}: the model could equally have produced
a different answer on a resample.
The \emph{ungrounded rate} is the fraction of samples that are both
wrong and weakly supported by retrieval evidence
($p_{\text{ground,max}} \leq 0.30$): the answer is not actively
refuted but is not entailed either.
The \emph{contradiction rate} is the fraction of samples that are
both wrong and flagged by the ensemble-max contradiction signal
($p_{\text{contra}} \geq 0.50$), measuring failures that slipped past
the contradiction veto.
The three sub-type rates are computed over the full evaluation
stream rather than over the confident-error slice, so that they
surface failure-mode prevalence independently of the storage gate;
they therefore may overlap each other and overlap with the pooled
confident-error rate rather than partitioning it. Their relative
movement across cycles indicates which failure mode each
architectural mechanism attenuates, while only the pooled
confident-error rate carries the pre-registered pass/fail commitment.
All four quantities are computed by the generic helper
\texttt{confabulation\_rate} with different
\texttt{(signal, threshold, direction)} triples.
The pooled rate is further decomposed in \cref{tab:halluc} into
an in-distribution (ID) component over the three benchmarks whose
verified episodes fed training (Natural Questions, TriviaQA, FEVER)
and a held-out (OOD) component over the three benchmarks never
seen during training (TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, ARC-Challenge).
Together the ID and OOD columns audit whether any observed
CE reduction is rote consolidation on training-adjacent
distributions or genuine transfer of the architectural mechanisms
(verified memory, confidence-aware routing, constrained
self-improvement under the $L_2$ anchor) to unseen factual-QA
distributions. The pre-registered $\geq 40\%$ floor remains on the
all-six pooled rate; the ID/OOD split is reported for
transfer-learning interpretation only.

\paragraph{Grounding (Table~\ref{tab:grounding}).}
Two grounding diagnostics isolate the retrieval pathway.
\emph{Unsupported-correct rate} is the fraction of
$\mathrm{EM}=1$ samples with $p_{\text{ground,max}} < 0.30$ -- correct
answers that retrieval evidence did not support.
\emph{Ungrounded-assertion rate} is the fraction of all samples with
$p_{\text{ground,max}} \leq 0.30$ and $\ustored \geq 0.50$ -- the
evaluation-side mirror of the Stage~5 early-exit confabulation gate
($u_\text{internal} \geq 0.70$ and
$p_{\text{ground,max}} \leq 0.20$) generalised so the same definition
serves both the online gate in~\cref{sec:system-overview} and
this offline diagnostic. The first quantifies parametric-memory
reliance; the second quantifies confidently-unsupported output
regardless of correctness.

\paragraph{Data purity (Table~\ref{tab:purity}).}
The verifier-decision breakdown -- counts and fractions of
\textsc{STORE}, \textsc{DEFER}, \textsc{ABSTAIN}, and
\textsc{DISCARD} decisions per cycle -- is reported alongside the
empirical verifier true-positive rate $\alpha_c$, the base generation
accuracy $p_c$, the theorem's predicted post-storage purity
$P_{\text{theory},c} = p_c\alpha_c / (p_c\alpha_c + (1-p_c)(1-\alpha_c))$,
and the measured purity $P_{\text{obs},c}$ for every cycle $c \in
\{0, 1, \ldots, 10\}$ (eleven rows). This table is the empirical
instantiation of the purity theorem from~\cref{sec:purity-theorem};
all five quantities are computed from the same purity-validation split
defined in~\cref{sec:benchmarks}. The \textsc{STORE} denominator is
obtained from \texttt{decision\_breakdown}. The chapter quotes the
Cycle-10 steady-state $\alpha_{10}$ as the defense-grade single
number (the verifier's balanced accuracy under the final
fine-tuned model) because it reflects the deployed system; the full
eleven-row trajectory is the audit that the theorem's pass condition
$\alpha_c > \tfrac{1}{2} \forall c$ from~\cref{sec:purity-theorem}
Remark~\ref{rem:alpha-empirical-scope} is met at every cycle. An
empty $|P_\text{theory}-P_\text{obs}|$ cell at any cycle is a
failure flag and is discussed in the results narrative rather than
being silently averaged.

\paragraph{Continual learning (Table~\ref{tab:continual}).}
Forgetting is reported with two instruments, following the dual-metric
design in~\cref{sec:self-improvement}. \emph{MMLU retention} is the
ratio of Cycle-$n$ MMLU accuracy (200 questions, shuffled seed~42
across the 57 subjects) to the Cycle-$0$ pristine-model baseline; it
is domain-neutral and never trained on, and is the headline forgetting
metric. In addition, we report \emph{backward transfer} and
\emph{forward transfer} following the gradient-episodic-memory
convention of Lopez-Paz and
Ranzato~\cite{lopezpaz2017gradient}.
Backward transfer averages $R_{T,i} - R_{i,i}$ over earlier
benchmarks $i$; positive values indicate that later cycles improved
earlier benchmarks, and negative values indicate catastrophic
forgetting. Forward transfer averages $R_{i-1,i} - \bar b_i$ relative
to the Cycle-$0$ baseline and measures whether learning on earlier
cycles helps benchmarks not yet reached.
Reporting both in addition to MMLU retention answers
the per-benchmark-per-cycle drift question that MMLU retention (being
a single scalar) cannot address.

\paragraph{Metric scoring caveats (TruthfulQA, MMLU).}
\label{sec:metric-scoring-caveats}
Two headline metrics in the panel are computed with deliberately
simplified offline proxies rather than the leaderboard protocols, for
reasons of reproducibility and compute cost. First, TruthfulQA is
scored with ROUGE-L against the reference-answer list (threshold
$0.15$; \cref{sec:benchmarks}), not with the GPT-judge fine-tuned on
human truthful/informative labels used by Lin et
al.~\cite{lin-etal-2022-truthfulqa}. The ROUGE-L proxy is deterministic
and independent of paid API access, so \caem{}'s absolute TruthfulQA
scores in \cref{tab:headline} are \emph{not directly comparable} to
published GPT-judge TruthfulQA leaderboard numbers; only cycle-over-cycle
deltas on the same proxy (e.g., ``\caem{} cycle 5 versus cycle 0'' on
TruthfulQA) and the same-proxy \caem{}-versus-baseline comparisons in
\cref{tab:sig-test} are valid. Second, MMLU is scored by constrained
generation (argmax over the first letter A/B/C/D under a CoT prompt), not
by log-likelihood ranking as in Hendrycks et
al.~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring} and the \texttt{lm-eval-harness}
convention. Generation-mode scoring typically runs three to eight points
below log-likelihood scoring on the same model because it is sensitive
to parsing failure; this choice is justified because the retention
instrument in \cref{tab:continual} and the RET axis of the CES report a
\emph{ratio} ($\rho$ = cycle-$n$ / cycle-$0$ on the same pipeline), not
an absolute leaderboard number. Absolute MMLU accuracy in
\cref{tab:headline} should therefore be read as a within-thesis
calibration point and not compared directly to published MMLU
accuracies.

\paragraph{Verifier backend and the empirical $\alpha$ assumption.}
The purity, monotonicity, and convergence theorems of
\cref{sec:theoretical-analysis} hold on any operating point for which
the verifier balanced accuracy $\alpha$ exceeds one half on the
distribution of claims actually scored. The distribution of claims
scored by the \caem{} verifier is language model generations, not
human written natural language inference pairs; generic NLI models
such as \texttt{roberta-large-mnli}, trained on MultiNLI and SNLI, have
been shown to miscalibrate on this shifted distribution, in the worst
case attaining a one hundred percent false positive rate at ninety
five percent recall on language model
hallucinations~\cite{semanticillusion2025}, which corresponds to
$\alpha \le 0.475$ and falsifies the theorem's premise. \caem{}'s
primary verifier backend is MiniCheck-Flan-T5-Large~\cite{tang2024minicheck},
fine tuned on language model generated claim support data rather than
on human sentence pairs, and the results in this chapter are reported
under that backend unless otherwise stated. An ablation row comparing
MiniCheck against legacy \texttt{roberta-large-mnli} is reported as
part of the verifier calibration diagnostic in
\cref{sec:verification-balanced-accuracy} and in the Cycle 0 baseline
table (\cref{tab:sig-test}). The cycle by cycle $\alpha$ trajectory is
reported alongside $|P_{\text{theory}} - P_{\text{obs}}|$ in
\cref{tab:purity}, so that the theorem's empirical premise is
audited per cycle rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Unified efficacy (CAEM Efficacy Score).}
Because a reviewer must read the seven tables above quickly, we report
a single composite scalar -- the \emph{\caem{} Efficacy Score} (CES) --
as the geometric mean of five orthogonal axes:
\[
\mathrm{CES} = \bigl(\mathrm{ACC} \cdot \mathrm{EPI} \cdot \mathrm{RET}
                    \cdot \mathrm{CAL} \cdot \mathrm{VER}\bigr)^{1/5},
\]
with $\mathrm{ACC}$ the mean per-benchmark correctness across all
six benchmarks (using the benchmark-appropriate metric: EM, F1,
label accuracy, or ROUGE-L, per \cref{tab:ces-axes}),
$\mathrm{EPI} = 1 - \text{(pooled confident-error rate)}$,
$\mathrm{RET} = \min(\text{MMLU retention ratio},\,1)$,
$\mathrm{CAL} = 1 - 2\min(\mathrm{ECE},\,0.5)$,
and $\mathrm{VER} = \max(2\beta - 1,\,0)$ where $\beta$ is the
verifier's balanced accuracy on labelled validation data. Each
component is clamped to $[0.01, 1]$ so that a transient failure on
one axis does not zero the score, but the geometric form ensures
that a genuine collapse on any axis (for instance, catastrophic
forgetting destroying $\mathrm{RET}$) pulls CES down rather than
being averaged away. CES is a thesis-introduced aggregate used
\emph{only} as a visualisation and headline scalar; the seven
component tables remain the authoritative quantitative record, and
CES is always reported with all five constituent values.

\paragraph{Statistical significance (Table~\ref{tab:sig-test}).}
All accuracy comparisons between \caem{} and baselines are tested
for significance using McNemar's
test~\cite{mcnemar1947note} with the Edwards continuity correction,
applied to paired per-question correct/incorrect outcomes. Non-parametric
$95\%$ confidence intervals for EM are computed by bootstrap resampling
with $B = 1000$ resamples. Both computations use the existing
\texttt{mcnemar\_test} and \texttt{bootstrap\_ci} helpers in
\texttt{eval/metrics.py}, which were written and tested before the
experiment phase.

Because \caem{} is compared against up to eight baselines across the
six factual-QA benchmarks (MMLU is a retention control, not a
baseline-comparison target), the raw test count is at most
$8 \times 6 = 48$ pairwise McNemar tests per cycle, and a naive
$p < 0.05$ threshold would inflate the family-wise error rate to
approximately $1 - 0.95^{48} \approx 0.91$. We therefore apply the
Holm--Bonferroni step-down correction~\cite{holm1979simple} within
each benchmark family: the per-baseline \caem{}-vs-baseline
$p$-values within a benchmark are sorted in ascending order, and
the $k$-th smallest is compared against $0.05 / (m - k + 1)$ where
$m$ is the number of baselines actually present in the
\texttt{outputs/baselines/} directory at the time
\texttt{scripts/baseline\_sig\_tests.py} is run (and hence the number
of tests within each benchmark family). For Phase 1A, Plan A runs
$m = 7$ inference + training baselines (B1--B5, B6, B7); the
STaR ceiling reference (B8) is executed only if budget allows
and, when absent, is not padded into the correction. This
is uniformly more powerful than plain Bonferroni while preserving the
family-wise error rate at $\alpha_{\text{fam}} = 0.05$, and is the
accepted correction for structured pairwise-comparison panels of
this size. A result is marked ``significant'' in
Table~\ref{tab:sig-test} only when it survives this step-down
correction; results that are directionally positive but fail the
correction are reported with both the raw and corrected $p$-values
so the reader can distinguish a suggestive trend from a confirmed
effect.

We separately require a minimum practical-significance effect size of
$\Delta \mathrm{EM} \geq 0.02$ (absolute, two percentage points on a
$500$-sample benchmark). McNemar's statistical test has high power at
this sample size and will flag effects as small as $\Delta \approx 0.01$
as significant, but differences below two points are within the cycle-
to-cycle variation \caem{} itself exhibits on a held-out seed, so we
do not claim architectural advantage below that threshold even when
the test is significant. Table~\ref{tab:sig-test} therefore reports
two markers: $\dagger$ for Holm-corrected $p < 0.05$, and $\star$ for
the conjunction of Holm-corrected $p < 0.05$ \emph{and}
$\Delta \mathrm{EM} \geq 0.02$. The thesis's architectural claims are
licensed only by the $\star$ marker; the $\dagger$ marker identifies
directionally positive effects whose magnitude is too small for the
claim to be practically meaningful, even though the underlying
comparison is statistically sound.

Where \caem{} loses a comparison (a baseline's EM exceeds \caem{}'s on
some benchmark), we apply the same two-stage rule in reverse: a
baseline is reported as strictly superior to \caem{} on a benchmark
only if $\Delta \mathrm{EM} \leq -0.02$ with Holm-corrected
$p < 0.05$. This symmetric framing prevents the write-up from silently
suppressing the baseline-wins case, which is a common failure mode
in architectural evaluations and one that the ablation
discussion~(\S\ref{sec:ablation-results}) treats as legitimate
evidence rather than as noise to be explained away.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The seven metric categories above answer seven distinct evaluation
questions: \emph{Is \caem{} more accurate and faster than its
baselines?} (Table~\ref{tab:headline});
\emph{Are \caem{}'s confidence signals well-calibrated?}
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration});
\emph{Has \caem{} reduced confident errors across every hallucination
sub-type?} (Table~\ref{tab:halluc});
\emph{Is \caem{}'s retrieval pathway earning its slot?}
(Table~\ref{tab:grounding});
\emph{Does the purity theorem's prediction match measurement?}
(Table~\ref{tab:purity});
\emph{Does self-improvement proceed without catastrophic forgetting
in any direction?} (Table~\ref{tab:continual});
\emph{Are the claimed improvements statistically significant?}
(Table~\ref{tab:sig-test}).
CES provides the single-scalar visualisation of these axes.
The remainder of this chapter populates each table in turn.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\caem{} is compared against eight external baselines spanning the
three families of prior systems identified in
\cref{sec:external-baselines}: prompt-level reasoning, retrieval
augmentation, and training-time self-improvement. All eight share the
\mbox{Flan-T5-Large} (780M) backbone wherever a backbone swap would not
invalidate the method, and all eight are run on the same six-benchmark
factual-QA panel with the same 500-sample draw per benchmark, so any
difference in score isolates the contribution of the architectural
component that distinguishes the baseline from \caem{}.

\begin{description}
  \item[B1: Zero-shot Flan-T5-Large.]
  The same 780M-parameter backbone \caem{} fine-tunes, invoked with no
  CoT prefix, no retrieval, and no verification. This is the empirical
  floor: any gain \caem{} reports must exceed this score, otherwise the
  gain is attributable to model capacity alone.

  \item[B2: Chain-of-Thought.]
  Flan-T5-Large with the ``Let's think step by step'' prefix
  \cite{wei2022chain}, no retrieval, no verification. Isolates the
  contribution of intermediate reasoning on the same backbone.

  \item[B3: DPR-RAG.]
  Dense Passage Retrieval \cite{karpukhin-etal-2020-dense} with
  $k=5$ Wikipedia passages concatenated into a 384-token context and
  generated from the same Flan-T5-Large backbone
  \cite{NEURIPS2020_6b493230}. Isolates the contribution of external
  grounding without any verification or memory.

  \item[B4: CoT + DPR-RAG.]
  B3 with the CoT prefix prepended. Isolates the contribution of
  reasoning over retrieved evidence.

  \item[B5: FLARE.]
  Active retrieval-augmented generation \cite{jiang-etal-2023-active}
  with confidence floor $\theta = 0.4$ and a 64-token look-ahead
  window, using the same DPR index as B3 and B4. Isolates the
  contribution of confidence-gated retrieval, which is the closest
  prior mechanism to \caem{}'s tier routing; the B5-versus-\caem{}
  gap measures whether three tiers and the nine-signal composite
  meaningfully improve on a single-tier confidence gate.

  \item[B6: Vanilla fine-tuning.]
  Flan-T5-Large trained for the same ten cycles as \caem{} on the
  verified episode pool, but without the $L_2$ anchor, without the
  MMLU retention guard, and without the episodic memory or the
  verifier at inference time, so every selected episode is treated as
  a clean label. Isolates the contribution of \caem{}'s
  retention-protection machinery.

  \item[B7: EWC-only fine-tuning.]
  B6 with the $L_2$ anchor and the MMLU retention guard added
  \cite{doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114} but with the verifier, the
  nine-signal composite, the memory, and the routing all still
  stripped. Isolates the contribution of the
  verification-and-memory machinery once retention machinery is held
  equal.

  \item[B8: Self-RAG (citation-only).]
  Asai et al.~\cite{asai2024selfrag} is the most ambitious
  retrieve-and-critique pipeline in the prior literature but its
  critic tokens are tied to a LLaMA-family backbone. Re-targeting
  them to Flan-T5-Large would change enough of the system that the
  resulting number would measure backbone and prompt-engineering
  differences more than architectural differences, so Self-RAG is
  included as a positioning reference rather than as a replicated
  numerical baseline.
\end{description}

Optional ceiling reference: \textbf{STaR}
\cite{NEURIPS2022_639a9a17}, the pure iterative-refinement scheme
reviewed in \cref{sec:external-baselines}, is reported separately where
compute permits, and serves as an aspirational upper bound for what a
verification-free bootstrap can reach on the same backbone.

Each baseline lacks exactly one of \caem{}'s architectural components;
the pairwise comparisons in \cref{sec:main-results} isolate the
contribution of that component. Implementation details, shared backbone
model, and compute budget are given in~\cref{sec:impl-details}.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:impl-details}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

All results in this chapter are produced by a single implementation
run against a pinned set of dependencies and a fixed hardware
envelope. This subsection records the substrate so that any number
reported in \S\ref{sec:main-results}--\S\ref{sec:ablation-results} is
traceable to a specific model checkpoint, GPU, configuration file,
and random seed.

\paragraph{Backbone and supporting models.}
The generator is Flan-T5-Large~(\cite{JMLR:v25:23-0870}, $780$M
parameters), loaded at \texttt{bfloat16} precision. Sentence-level
embeddings for episodic memory keys, retrieval similarity, and
semantic-entropy clustering are produced by the frozen
\texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2} sentence
encoder~(\cite{reimers-gurevych-2019-sentence}, $110$M parameters,
$768$-dim output). NLI entailment and contradiction probabilities
feeding the verifier's nine-signal composite are produced by the
frozen \texttt{roberta-large-mnli} cross-encoder~(\cite{liu2019roberta},
$355$M parameters). The same three checkpoints are used by every
\caem{} variant and by every retrieval-family baseline (B3 DPR-RAG,
B4 CoT+RAG, B5 FLARE); backbone identity is held constant across the
comparison panel so that score differences isolate architectural
contributions rather than capacity differences.

\paragraph{Hardware.}
Cycle-level training and evaluation run on a single NVIDIA
RTX~4090 ($24$~GB VRAM) rented through Vast.ai, paired with a
Ryzen-class host CPU and $64$~GB of system RAM. The L$_2$ anchor
$\theta_{\text{prev}}$ co-resides on GPU with the training parameters
so that the anchor penalty can be computed without host-device
transfers each step, which doubles the effective weight footprint
during a cycle and motivates the \texttt{bfloat16} precision choice.
The episodic-memory FAISS index stays on GPU for vector search and
spills to CPU only for periodic save; the Wikipedia DPR passage index
($21$M vectors) is held on CPU throughout because its size exceeds
what can be co-resident with the generator on a single $24$~GB card.

\paragraph{Cycle structure and sample budgets.}
\caem{} is trained for $N = 10$ self-improvement cycles following the
schedule in \S\ref{sec:self-improvement}. At every cycle the
generator is evaluated on the full six-benchmark panel with
$n_{\text{eval}} = 500$ questions per benchmark drawn under the
fixed split assignments documented in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-overview}.
Four additional disjoint sample splits support the per-cycle
diagnostics: $n_{\text{cal}} = 500$ for temperature calibration and
reliability-diagram fitting, $n_{\text{purity}} = 500$ for the
verifier-balanced-accuracy estimate and the
$\lvert P_{\text{theory}} - P_{\text{obs}} \rvert$ comparison that
populates Table~\ref{tab:purity}, $n_{\text{MMLU}} = 200$ shuffled
across the 57 MMLU subjects for the retention guard, and
$n_{\text{screen}} = 1\,500$ for the Phase~1 screening sweep that
ranks the sixteen ablation variants before the
confirmatory panel. All five splits are non-overlapping within a
benchmark and the split assignment is fixed across cycles and across
all ablation variants. Source disjointness is additionally enforced
at the dataset-split level: the calibration fold $n_{\text{cal}}$,
the purity fold $n_{\text{purity}}$, and the training pool for the
self-improvement loop are drawn from the \emph{training splits} of
the three in-training benchmarks (FEVER, TriviaQA, and Natural
Questions) through \texttt{split\_calibration\_sets} in
\texttt{scripts/run\_experiment.py}, whereas the evaluation fold
$n_{\text{eval}}$ is drawn from the \emph{dev or test splits} of all
six benchmarks (FEVER dev, TriviaQA and Natural Questions validation,
TruthfulQA single split, StrategyQA test, ARC-Challenge test) through
\texttt{load\_eval\_transfer\_pool}. The two loaders therefore
consume disjoint source splits, and the calibration-fold fit for
$T^{*}$ and for the decision-tree quantile thresholds
(Section~\ref{sec:self-improvement} threshold-calibration paragraph)
never observes a question that will later feed the evaluation fold.
The MMLU retention probe $n_{\text{MMLU}}$ is drawn from MMLU, which
is not in either loader. The screening fold $n_{\text{screen}}$
samples from the training pool only and is disjoint from the
per-cycle calibration and purity folds within that pool. The three
transfer-eval benchmarks (TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, ARC-Challenge)
contribute zero samples to the calibration or purity folds and are
evaluated only, which is consistent with the
transfer-generalisation role assigned to them in Chapter 3.

\paragraph{Per-cycle calibration protocol.}
At every cycle boundary, after fine-tuning and retroactive
re-verification have completed but before the next cycle's online
routing begins, the temperature scalar $T$ is re-fitted by LBFGS on
NLL over the disjoint $n_{\text{cal}} = 500$ calibration split
following Guo et al.~\cite{pmlr-v70-guo17a}, because fine-tuning
shifts the generator's logit scale each
cycle~(\cite{ovadia2019trust,thulasidasan2019mixup}). $T$ is the
only runtime-active calibration surface; the $u_{\text{stored}}$
composite weights and all router thresholds
($\tau_{\text{store}}$, $\tau_{\text{train}}$,
$\lambda_{\text{route}}$, the OR-condition floor, and $\rho_{\min}$)
are fixed by design and are \emph{not} re-fitted.

\paragraph{Fixed thresholds and post-hoc sensitivity analysis.}
The five scalar thresholds that gate storage, training-pool admission,
router dispatch, the OR-condition override, and the forgetting
circuit-breaker ($\tau_{\text{store}} = 0.65$,
$\tau_{\text{train}} = 0.75$, $\lambda_{\text{route}} = 0.70$ with
$(1-\lambda_{\text{route}}) = 0.30$ for the memory-match term,
the pre-routing floor $u_{\text{pre}} \geq 0.60$, and
$\rho_{\min} = 0.93$) are set at project start using a combination of
literature priors and small calibration-split pilot estimates; see
\cref{sec:implementation} for the per-threshold provenance. Tuning
each threshold directly on the test split would compromise the
pre-registered evaluation protocol, so we adopt a post-hoc sensitivity
audit instead: once the main cyclic run completes, each of the five
thresholds is varied individually across a three-point grid
(approximately $\pm 10\%$ of its default) while holding all other
values fixed, and the resulting $\Delta\text{CES}$ is reported in
\cref{tab:threshold-sensitivity}. This design tests whether CES is
robust to small perturbations of the threshold vector without
optimising on the same evaluation data; sharp CES sensitivity to any
single threshold would be flagged in~\cref{sec:ablation-results} as a
finding that warrants threshold revisit in future work rather than as
a correction to the present thesis's claims. Threshold sensitivity
runs are executed after the main Vast.ai budget is spent, on lab-PC
hardware (RTX~4090/5090 where available) with Vast.ai as the
fallback path.

\paragraph{Cold-start memory.}
Cycle~$0$ begins with a pre-populated episodic store of $447$
verified episodes drawn from an 80/20 stratification of the three
in-domain training benchmarks (FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions)
under the same Stage~5 verifier used online, seeded with the pristine
Flan-T5-Large backbone. This is documented in detail in the
cold-start section of \S\ref{sec:self-improvement}; the role of the
$447$ episodes is to give Tier~1 retrieval a non-empty index on the
very first query of Cycle~$0$ so that the routing decision can be
exercised from the first sample rather than starting with an
inevitable Tier~2/Tier~3 fallback.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every cycle run is controlled by a single configuration dataclass
(\texttt{caem.config.CAEMConfig}) that records every numeric constant
in the system; each constant carries a literature~[LIT], design~[DES],
or calibration~[CAL] tag per~\S\ref{sec:implementation}, and the
dataclass asserts at load that no field is missing. Random seeds are
pinned at three levels: Python's \texttt{random} (\texttt{seed=42}),
NumPy (\texttt{np.random.seed(42)}), and PyTorch
(\texttt{torch.manual\_seed(42)}, \texttt{torch.cuda.manual\_seed\_all(42)}),
with \texttt{torch.backends.cudnn.deterministic = True} for
convolutional kernels. Benchmark splits are materialised once to
disk with a frozen sample ordering so that re-running Cycle-$n$ months
later under the same seed produces the same per-sample records. The
framework versions that any reported number is bound to are Python
$3.10$, PyTorch $2.1$, HuggingFace Transformers $4.36$, Datasets
$2.16$, sentence-transformers $2.3$, and \texttt{faiss-cpu} $1.7$.

\paragraph{Compute budget summary.}
One complete \caem{} run (Cycles~$0$--$10$ across the six benchmarks
plus MMLU retention, plus calibration and purity diagnostics) occupies
approximately $18$ GPU-hours on the RTX~4090 envelope. The external
baseline panel (\S\ref{sec:baselines}, B1--B7) adds approximately
$16$ GPU-hours sequentially ($\sim 3.5$ hours for the five inference
baselines B1--B5 combined, $\sim 12$ hours for the two training
baselines B6--B7 combined, each of which runs for the same $10$
cycles as \caem{}). The optional STaR ceiling reference adds a further
$\sim 8$ GPU-hours when compute permits; Self-RAG is included as a
positioning reference rather than a replicated numerical baseline
(\S\ref{sec:baselines}, B8) and contributes no GPU time. The Phase~1
screening sweep over the seventeen variants at three
cycles and $n_{\text{screen}} = 1\,500$ occupies a further $\sim 24$
GPU-hours before the confirmatory panel narrows to the subset that
earns full $10$-cycle runs.

\section{Ablation Methodology}
\label{sec:ablation}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

This section describes the ablation protocol that populates the
ablation table in \S\ref{sec:ablation-results}.

\paragraph{Training-time versus inference-time variants.}
\caem{} is a stateful system whose weights evolve across cycles.
An ablation that merely disables a mechanism at inference, while
evaluating on weights that were already \emph{shaped} by that mechanism
in previous cycles, does not measure the mechanism's contribution;
it measures a counterfactual confound. We therefore classify every
ablation variant as either \emph{inference-time} (the mechanism can be
flipped off without changing training history, so the reference-run
weights may be reused) or \emph{training-time} (the mechanism alters
storage admission, verification decisions, the self-improvement loss,
or any other signal that feeds back into subsequent cycles, so the
variant requires its own full cyclic rerun from the pristine baseline).
Under strict application of this principle, only two variants are
inference-time: the full system and
\texttt{no\_self\_improvement}; all 15 remaining variants are
training-time and obtain their own 10-cycle trajectory.

\paragraph{Variant registry.}
A named registry of 17 variants is defined in
\texttt{caem/ablation/variants.py} and dispatched by
\texttt{scripts/run\_cyclic\_ablation.py}. Each cyclic rerun writes its
cycle-wise CES axes to
\texttt{outputs/ablation/<variant>/seed\_<N>/ces\_axes\_per\_cycle.json},
and \texttt{scripts/aggregate\_ablation.py} assembles the per-variant
rows for the ablation table. Table~\ref{tab:ablation-registry}
summarises the registry.

\paragraph{Two-phase sweep protocol.}
Running all 14 training-time variants at the confirmatory
configuration ($n=5000$ SIL per cycle, 10 cycles, 3 seeds) exceeds the
project's self-funded compute envelope. The study therefore uses a
two-phase protocol.\footnote{The screening pass is a methodological
instrument to allocate confirmatory budget to the highest-$\Delta$CES
candidates; screening runs are not reported in the main ablation table.
Variant selection is pre-registered in
\texttt{caem-implementation-log.md} (Session~44) before the confirmatory
pass begins.} Phase~1 (self-funded, approximately USD~200) executes a
screening pass at $n=1500$ SIL for 3~cycles on a single seed (42) across
the entire 17-variant registry, ranks variants by point-estimate
$\Delta$CES, and then commits budget to the confirmatory pass at
$n=5000$ SIL for 10~cycles on a single seed for the top candidates
plus the reference run. Phase~2 (post-funding, approximately
+USD~700) re-runs the confirmatory pass for seeds 123 and 456 across
every cyclic variant, enabling mean$\pm$std reporting and
within-variant McNemar tests. Phase~1 results will be disclosed as
point estimates with an explicit single-seed limitation paragraph in
\S\ref{sec:ablation-results}; Phase~2 tables, once collected, will
supersede them with variance-bearing reports.

\paragraph{Metric reported.}
Each variant row reports the five CES axes
(ACC, EPI, RET, CAL, VER) and the geometric-mean scalar CES at the
final cycle of its own trajectory, plus $\Delta\text{CES}$ relative to
the reference run with independent-seeds standard-deviation propagation
once Phase~2 data is available.\footnote{Error bars on
$\Delta\text{CES}$ are propagated as
$\sigma_{\Delta} = \sqrt{\sigma_v^{2} + \sigma_{\text{full}}^{2}}$,
which is exact under seed-independence between the variant and
reference runs. Phase~2 guarantees this by construction: each variant
uses seeds \{42, 123, 456\} drawn independently of the reference run's
seed set, so no run is shared across the two std terms. If any seed
were reused, the two variances would become positively correlated and
this formula would over-estimate $\sigma_{\Delta}$.} The per-cycle CES
trace is also plotted for the reference run and for the ablation
variants whose $\Delta$CES pattern across cycles is diagnostic
(typically \texttt{no\_self\_improvement}, \texttt{no\_verifier}, and
\texttt{no\_retroverify}).

\subsubsection*{Expected effects and falsifiable predictions}

We record below the expected sign, magnitude, and primary CES axis
for each variant \emph{before} the Phase~1 runs complete. This
pre-registration commits the hypothesis register to paper so that the
Phase~1 result discussion in~\cref{sec:ablation-results} can be read as
confirmation, partial confirmation, or falsification against a fixed
set of predictions rather than as a post-hoc rationalisation fitted to
the data. The predictions group into four mechanism clusters
(self-improvement machinery, memory and routing, verification signals,
threshold sensitivity) and are stated at the level of
$\Delta\text{CES}$ bands ($<0.01$ = negligible, $0.01$--$0.05$ =
moderate, $>0.05$ = large) and primary axis attribution.

\paragraph{Self-improvement
(\texttt{no\_self\_improvement}, \texttt{no\_retroverify}).}
\texttt{no\_self\_improvement} skips Stage~8 fine-tuning entirely and
reports only the Cycle-0 evaluation; it is the strictest test of the
self-improvement loop's cumulative contribution. We predict a large
negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ (at least $-0.05$) driven primarily by the
ACC axis, with the per-cycle CES trace flat rather than rising,
because every later cycle's improvement in the reference run is by
construction attributable to training. Falsification condition: if
\texttt{no\_self\_improvement} matches the reference CES within
$0.01$, the reference trajectory's rise is not coming from
fine-tuning and the thesis's central engineering claim fails.
\texttt{no\_retroverify} disables the end-of-cycle retroactive
re-verification pass; we predict a moderate negative
$\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.02$ to $-0.04$) driven by EPI and VER (the
verifier balanced accuracy $\alpha$ stops improving across cycles
because the re-verification signal no longer folds back into the
pool), with the purity trajectory in Figure~\ref{fig:purity-trajectory}
exhibiting a visibly shallower slope than the reference. The
$L_2$-anchor strength $\lambda$ and the $\rho_{\min}$ forgetting
circuit-breaker are held at their design values across every cyclic
variant; the post-hoc threshold-sensitivity sweep
(Table~\ref{tab:threshold-sensitivity}) is the instrument that
probes their individual contributions, not a separate ablation row.

\paragraph{Verification and gating
(\texttt{no\_verifier}, \texttt{no\_grounding},
\texttt{no\_contradiction\_veto}, \texttt{no\_store\_gate},
\texttt{aggressive\_store}).}
\texttt{no\_verifier} bypasses the UnifiedVerifier and admits every
Tier-2/3 generation directly into the memory pool; it is the hardest
test of the verifier's pool-purification role. We predict a large
negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.06$ or worse) with the EPI and VER
axes collapsing and the $P_{\text{obs}}$ column in
Table~\ref{tab:purity} falling toward $p$ rather than rising above it;
this is the direct empirical mirror of Theorem~\ref{thm:purity}'s
prediction that purification requires $\alpha > \tfrac{1}{2}$ and
that a chance-equivalent verifier contributes no purification.
\texttt{no\_grounding} zeroes the three external-grounding weights in
$\ustored$ (the two Wikipedia $p_{\text{ground}}$ signals and the
NLI entailment probability) and renormalises the surviving three
signals; we expect a moderate negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.03$ to
$-0.05$) concentrated on EPI and VER on the retrieval-dependent
benchmarks (TriviaQA, Natural Questions), with a smaller effect on
benchmarks answerable from parametric memory. If the $\Delta\text{CES}$
band exceeds the $-0.05$ floor, the grounding-family signals are
dominating the composite more than the $0.30 + 0.15 + 0.15 = 0.60$
weight share would suggest and the calibration design should be
revisited. \texttt{no\_contradiction\_veto} neutralises the
$p_{\text{contra}}$ veto so that contradicting generations may still
reach STORE. We predict a moderate negative EPI effect ($-0.02$ to
$-0.04$) concentrated on the contradiction sub-type row of
Table~\ref{tab:halluc}; the pooled confident-error rate should rise
visibly, and the contradiction-specific row should rise more sharply
than the confabulation or ungrounded rows. \texttt{no\_store\_gate}
drops both $\tau_{\text{store}}$ and $\tau_{\text{defer}}$ to zero,
admitting every generation to the pool regardless of $\ustored$; we
predict a large negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.05$ or worse) with
a visible collapse of $P_{\text{obs}}$ toward $p$ that is diagnostic
of the storage-gate contribution separate from the verifier's.
\texttt{aggressive\_store} is the opposite-direction companion: it
raises $\tau_{\text{store}}$ to $0.85$ and $\tau_{\text{defer}}$ to
$0.75$, admitting roughly a quarter of the default pool per cycle. We
expect a small negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.01$ to $-0.03$) with
ACC falling because the training pool is too small to cover the
distribution, but $P_{\text{obs}}$ rising because the surviving
episodes are more tightly verified; a positive
$\Delta\text{CES}$ here would mean the default
$\tau_{\text{store}} = 0.65$ is admitting noise and should be
revisited. \texttt{roberta\_nli\_backend} swaps the verifier judge
back to generic \texttt{roberta-large-mnli}, testing the counterfactual
that a natural-language-inference model trained on human MultiNLI /
SNLI pairs could ground the purity theorem's $\alpha > \tfrac{1}{2}$
premise on language-model-generated claims. Literature predicts this
fails: HaluEval~2025 reports a one-hundred-percent false-positive
rate at ninety-five-percent recall for a near-state-of-the-art generic
NLI model on LM hallucinations, corresponding to $\alpha \le 0.475$
and a zero VER-axis contribution after the $\max(2\beta-1,\,0)$
clamp. We therefore predict a moderate negative
$\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.02$ to $-0.05$) concentrated primarily on the
VER axis, with a secondary smaller EPI drop because the generic NLI
verifier conflates fluent-but-wrong claims with fluent positives and
admits them to the pool. Falsification condition: a zero or positive
$\Delta\text{CES}$ here against the MiniCheck reference would mean
the MiniCheck swap is not load-bearing for the thesis claim and the
thesis main-text case (\S\ref{sec:metric-scoring-caveats} verifier
backend and the empirical $\alpha$ assumption) should be revised
empirically rather than dropped. This variant's role is to convert
the one-shot calibration diagnostic of
\S\ref{sec:metric-scoring-caveats} into trajectory evidence across
ten cycles so that the backend choice is defended at the cycle-level
rather than only at the Cycle-0 reliability-diagram level.

\paragraph{Calibration and signal mix
(\texttt{no\_internal\_calibration}, \texttt{no\_semantic\_entropy},
\texttt{equal\_signal\_weights}).}
\texttt{no\_internal\_calibration} zeroes the $u_{\text{internal}}$
weight (token probability + MC Dropout) in $\ustored$ and rescales the
surviving five signals; we predict a small-to-moderate negative
$\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.01$ to $-0.03$) driven primarily by CAL, with
the reliability diagram at Cycle~$N$ showing a visibly wider departure
from the $y=x$ line in the high-confidence tail where internal-calibration
disagreement is most informative. \texttt{no\_semantic\_entropy} drops
the Farquhar-2024 signal from the composite; we predict a similar band
($-0.01$ to $-0.03$) with the confabulation sub-type row in
Table~\ref{tab:halluc} rising sharply, because semantic entropy is the
dominant detector of confabulations in the literature. If
\texttt{no\_semantic\_entropy} shows a \emph{smaller} effect than
\texttt{no\_internal\_calibration}, the $0.10$ default weight assigned
to semantic entropy is too low and the composite should be rebalanced.
\texttt{equal\_signal\_weights} flattens the six-signal composite from
the default $0.30/0.15/0.15/0.15/0.15/0.10$ to uniform $1/6$ each; we
predict a neutral-to-slightly-negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($0$ to
$-0.02$) because the default weights already lie near the mean of the
admissible simplex, but the VER axis should drop more visibly than the
CES headline would suggest. A large positive $\Delta\text{CES}$ under
uniform weights would mean the designed weighting is actively harmful,
which is the falsification path for Chapter~4's weight-calibration
narrative.

\paragraph{Safety gate (\texttt{no\_early\_exit}).}
\texttt{no\_early\_exit} defuses the confabulation gate
($u_{\text{internal}} \geq 0.70 \wedge p_{\text{ground,max}} \leq 0.20
\rightarrow \textsc{Discard}$) so that confabulations reach the
four-outcome decision tree and may enter the deferred buffer or the
store. We predict a moderate negative EPI-axis effect ($-0.02$ to
$-0.04$) concentrated on the confabulation sub-type row of
Table~\ref{tab:halluc}, with a visibly flatter purity trajectory in
early cycles where the early-exit gate is most active. The
confabulation-rate column is the primary diagnostic here; the
aggregate CES delta will understate the gate's importance because
many confabulations that slip past the early-exit gate are still
caught downstream by the $u_{\text{stored}}$ composite.

\paragraph{Routing (\texttt{no\_tier1}).}
\texttt{no\_tier1} forces every query through Stage~4+ (no Tier-1
shortcut) by raising the Tier-1 combined-score threshold above $1.0$;
we predict a moderate negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.02$ to $-0.04$)
driven jointly by ACC (memory-answerable queries now route to noisier
tiers) and latency (not a CES axis but reported in
Table~\ref{tab:headline}); the effect should grow over cycles as the
reference memory accumulates more high-$\ustored$ hits. The routing
distribution row in Table~\ref{tab:routing} is the primary diagnostic:
the reference-run Tier-1 fraction rising from $\sim 10$--$20\%$ at
Cycle~0 to $\sim 35$--$55\%$ at Cycle~$N$ should all shift to Tier-2/3
under \texttt{no\_tier1}.

\paragraph{Retrieval and memory
(\texttt{no\_tier3\_rag}, \texttt{no\_novelty\_filter},
\texttt{no\_recency\_decay}).}
\texttt{no\_tier3\_rag} disables Tier-3 Wikipedia RAG and falls back
to zero-shot generation on queries that would have used retrieval;
we predict a large negative EPI and grounding-axis effect on
benchmarks that rely on out-of-parametric knowledge (primarily
TriviaQA and Natural Questions), with a smaller effect on benchmarks
answerable from the base model's parametric memory (FEVER,
StrategyQA, ARC-C). The unsupported-correct rate in
Table~\ref{tab:grounding} should \emph{rise}, not fall, because the
remaining correct answers are entirely parametric and carry no
retrieval support by definition. \texttt{no\_novelty\_filter} permits
duplicate episodes by relaxing the $0.95$ cosine-novelty threshold; we
predict a small positive or neutral $\Delta\text{CES}$ on the
benchmark panel combined with rising memory size and rising retrieval
latency. The memory-growth curve in Table~\ref{tab:memory-growth} is
the primary cost-side diagnostic; this is an ablation whose headline
``result'' is the growth trajectory rather than a CES delta.
\texttt{no\_recency\_decay} zeroes the recency term in the Value score
so that pruning becomes pure importance ranking; we predict a small
negative $\Delta\text{CES}$ ($-0.01$ to $-0.02$) with the effect
concentrated on benchmarks whose answer distribution drifts across
cycles, because without the recency term old-and-important episodes
crowd out newer-and-also-important ones.

\paragraph{Interpreting the full ablation pattern.}
The above predictions are deliberately specific about sign, axis, and
magnitude band so that the Phase~1 result discussion can be graded
against them rather than narrated around them. Three macro outcomes
are the diagnostic cases. If most predictions land within their
bands, the hypothesis register is confirmed and the CES composite is
reading the system in the way its design intended. If a minority of
predictions are inverted (for instance, \texttt{no\_tier3\_rag}
showing a small rather than large EPI drop on a benchmark where we
predicted large), the specific mechanism's contribution is
over-stated in the thesis and the Chapter~6 limitations paragraph
should call out the correction explicitly. If the reference
trajectory collapses under \texttt{no\_self\_improvement} or
\texttt{no\_verifier} by less than $0.05$ in $\Delta\text{CES}$,
the thesis's central engineering claim requires substantial
revision; this is the high-stakes falsification path, and its
outcome fixes what the viva defence needs to anticipate.

\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

This section reports the seven metric-category tables introduced in
\cref{sec:metrics}, together with the three empirical validations of
the theoretical claims stated in~\cref{sec:theoretical-analysis}.
Each table below carries a caption and label already referenced from
the methodology paragraphs; numbers are populated from the
confirmatory-pass outputs at Cycle~$N$ and are reported with 95\%
confidence intervals where applicable.

\subsubsection*{Expected results and pre-registered predictions.}
As with the ablation sweep, we commit the expected shape of each
headline table before Phase~1 confirmatory runs begin, so that the
cross-seed outcome is judged against fixed hypotheses rather than
ex-post rationalisation. Predictions are stated as qualitative
direction plus numeric bands on the primary axis of each table; we
treat any outcome outside the predicted band as a falsification
signal that must be explained in~\cref{sec:ablation-results} and
reconciled with the theorems of~\cref{sec:theoretical-analysis}.

\paragraph{Headline accuracy (\cref{tab:headline}).}
On the three training benchmarks (FEVER, TriviaQA, NaturalQuestions)
we predict pooled EM gains of $+0.03$ to $+0.08$ over the strongest
external baseline at Cycle~$N$, with the largest gap on TriviaQA and
NaturalQuestions where episodic retrieval is expected to dominate and
a smaller gap on FEVER where the strong CoT baseline already
exploits structured claim verification. Decomposed by tier,
$\mathrm{EM}_{T_1} > \mathrm{EM}_{T_2} > \mathrm{EM}_{T_3}$ is
expected to hold at every cycle, since Tier~1 answers a queries that
the router has already judged high-confidence. A crossover in which
$\mathrm{EM}_{T_2}$ or $\mathrm{EM}_{T_3}$ exceeds $\mathrm{EM}_{T_1}$
would indicate router miscalibration and triggers the threshold
sensitivity review in~\cref{sec:ablation-results}. On the three
transfer-eval benchmarks (TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, ARC-Challenge) we
predict modest positive transfer ($+0.01$ to $+0.03$ EM over the
strongest baseline) but do not treat a near-zero transfer effect as a
falsification: transfer-eval benchmarks test whether SIL-trained
episodic content generalises off-distribution, not whether SIL
improves in-distribution accuracy.

\paragraph{Calibration (\cref{tab:calibration}).}
We predict monotone improvement in ECE and Brier score across cycles
$0,\ldots,N$, with ECE at Cycle~$N$ landing in the $0.03$--$0.06$
band (against a Cycle~$0$ baseline we expect near $0.10$--$0.15$).
AUROC of $\ustored$ against correctness is predicted in the
$0.70$--$0.82$ band at Cycle~$N$. A non-monotone ECE trajectory, or
an AUROC at Cycle~$N$ below $0.65$, would indicate that verification
signals are drifting under self-improvement and would open the
question of whether EWC anchoring is insufficient to protect the
calibration head. The reliability diagram in~\cref{fig:reliability}
should show the bin-wise accuracy curve tracking the $y = x$ identity
line more closely at Cycle~$N$ than at Cycle~$0$, with any remaining
departure concentrated in the high-$\ustored$ tail.

\paragraph{Hallucination decomposition (\cref{tab:halluc}).}
The thesis pre-registers a relative reduction of at least $40\%$
in the pooled confident-error rate between Cycle~$0$ and Cycle~$N$
as the architectural pass/fail floor fixed in
\cref{sec:evaluation}, and reports the Cycle-by-Cycle achieved
value in \cref{tab:halluc} against that floor. The thesis does
\emph{not} commit to a specific expected value above the floor;
the floor is what the architectural claim owns, and an achieved
value below the floor on three independent seeds is reported as a
negative result on the architectural claim and feeds the
targeted-deficiency discussion in Chapter~6. Within the
decomposition we expect the largest absolute drop in the
\emph{confabulation} sub-type, because Tier-2 retrieval removes
the need to fabricate facts that exist in the episodic memory,
with smaller drops in the \emph{ungrounded} and
\emph{contradiction} sub-type rates. A pattern in which the
contradiction rate \emph{rises} across cycles would be a serious
warning sign for verifier drift and would require rolling back a
cycle in the self-improvement loop before the analysis chapter is
written.
On the ID/OOD transfer-learning split, we expect both the ID and
the OOD pooled CE to fall across cycles: the ID drop reflects
straightforward consolidation on distributions whose verified
episodes fed the training pool, while the OOD drop reflects
transfer of the architectural mechanisms themselves (verified
memory populated at evaluation time, confidence-aware routing to
Tier-2 retrieval, and the calibration gains from $L_2$-anchored
fine-tuning) to benchmarks the model has never seen during
training. A pattern in which the ID CE falls sharply while the
OOD CE stays flat would indicate that the observed pooled
reduction is driven by rote consolidation rather than by
generalisation of the architecture, and would weaken the
architectural claim even where the $\geq 40\%$ pooled floor is
formally cleared; such an outcome would be discussed in
Chapter~6 alongside the headline verdict.

\paragraph{Routing distribution (\cref{tab:routing}).}
We predict the Tier~1 fraction to rise from a Cycle~$0$ cold-start
level of $10$--$20\%$ to a Cycle~$N$ steady-state level of
$35$--$55\%$, with a corresponding drop in the Tier~3 fraction. This
reflects the expected shift of verified episodes into the fast path as
the memory grows. The safety-override fraction should stay bounded,
ideally in the $2$--$8\%$ range: a steady rise in override use would
indicate a systematic gap between router confidence and true task
difficulty and would motivate revisiting the OR-condition in the
router design.

\paragraph{Verifier-signal attribution (\cref{tab:verifier-attrib}).}
We predict that NLI-entailment and semantic entropy will be the two
most informative individual signals, each with per-signal AUROC in
the $0.63$--$0.72$ range on pooled correctness. Self-consistency is
predicted to sit slightly below in the $0.58$--$0.66$ range, and the
token-probability signal is predicted to be the weakest individual
contributor. MC-Dropout contribution is treated as an open question:
if its standalone AUROC falls below $0.55$ the ablation in
\cref{sec:ablation-results} should indicate whether it is worth
keeping in the composite.

\paragraph{Nine-signal correlation structure and effective-count
analysis.}
\label{sec:signal-correlation}
The per-signal AUROCs above tell us which individual signals are
informative; a separate question is whether the nine-signal composite
has nine \emph{effectively independent} signals or substantially
fewer. \citet{valentin2024hallucination} publish the only prior
cross-signal Spearman heat-map in the LLM hallucination-detection
literature (ten signals, four benchmarks) and report that ensembling
collapses into three redundancy clusters. We follow the same
protocol on the six CAEM benchmarks using the per-sample signal values
exported by the evaluation harness, and predict the following
grouping on a priori grounds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High redundancy} ($|\rho| > 0.9$, drop candidates):
          (i) token-probability mean versus token-probability minimum,
          which collapse to the same value on the one-to-five-token
          answers typical of TriviaQA, NQ, and ARC; (ii) self-consistency
          semantic-cluster count versus normalised semantic entropy,
          since \citet{farquhar2024nature}'s discrete-SE variant is
          literally a cluster count over the same $K=10$ samples;
          (iii) NLI-entailment versus semantic-cluster on the sample-set
          signals, if NLI is evaluated on the same sample pool.
    \item \textbf{Moderate correlation} ($0.6 \le |\rho| \le 0.85$,
          retain): MC-Dropout variance against token-probability
          captures an epistemic-versus-aleatoric axis that
          \citet{vashurin2024polygraph} report as non-collinear on
          TriviaQA/Mistral-7B; retrieval top-1 similarity against
          top-$k$ dispersion is expected to be negatively correlated at
          $-0.5$ to $-0.7$ per the dense-retrieval literature, carrying
          absolute-match versus ambiguity as distinct signals.
    \item \textbf{Independent} ($|\rho| < 0.4$, retain without
          question): the three grounding signals against all generation
          signals, provided grounding uses the external Wikipedia
          index; \citet{valentin2024hallucination} specifically find
          NLI-DeBERTa the most independent signal in their set.
\end{itemize}
Post-pruning, the effective composite is predicted to reduce to
five-to-seven independent signals out of nine nominal. The full
nine-by-nine Spearman matrix per benchmark is produced by
\texttt{scripts/signal\_correlation\_matrix.py} from the evaluation
harness's per-sample signal values and is reported in
an appendix figure pair (one
aggregated across benchmarks, one per-benchmark array). Three falsification conditions are pre-registered: if no pair exceeds
$|\rho| = 0.9$ on any benchmark, the pruning plan is unmotivated
empirically and the thesis should retain all nine; if more than four
pairs exceed $|\rho| = 0.9$ aggregated across benchmarks, the
composite is substantially narrower than its nominal signal count
and the Chapter~4 composite formula of
Equation~\ref{eq:ustored-verify} should be re-derived from the
surviving independent axes; if the aggregated matrix matches the
Valentin 2024 clustering ordering within rank-$\rho$ distance of
$0.2$, the thesis can cross-cite the published ten-signal evidence
directly and strengthen the defensibility of the composite against
the ``why not just semantic entropy?'' question that
\citet{farquhar2024nature} raises on open-generation benchmarks and
that \citet{vashurin2024polygraph} shows is dominated by verbalised
UQ on constrained-output benchmarks such as MMLU. The
thirty-two-configuration singleton-plus-leave-one-out ablation design
of \citet{kuhn2023semantic} is a scheduled Phase 1 Full / TMLR
deliverable and is recorded as the ablation template against which
the registry in Table~\ref{tab:ablation-registry} was sized.

\paragraph{Episodic-memory growth (\cref{tab:memory-growth}).}
We predict sublinear growth in $|\mathcal{M}|$ across cycles, because
\textsc{Store} is the minority outcome once the memory is seeded.
By Cycle~$N$ we expect $|\mathcal{M}|$ to lie in the $1500$--$2500$
range, with the cumulative \textsc{Discard} rate dominating and
\textsc{Deferred} shrinking relative to early cycles as the verifier
calibrates. A runaway memory ($|\mathcal{M}| > 3000$) would suggest
the verification threshold is too permissive and would trigger the
purity-versus-growth trade-off discussion in Chapter~6.

\paragraph{CES composite (\cref{tab:ces}).}
We predict the CES at Cycle~$N$ to exceed the strongest external
baseline by $+0.04$ to $+0.10$, with the geometric-mean structure
ensuring that no single axis (ACC, EPI, RET, CAL, VER) can carry the
gain alone: every axis is predicted to improve over Cycle~$0$, with
ACC and EPI leading the delta, RET and CAL in the middle, and VER
contributing a smaller increment because its Cycle~$0$ value is
already boosted by the pre-trained NLI cross-encoder. Any axis that
regresses across cycles would be flagged as a targeted deficiency to
address in future work.

\paragraph{Grounding diagnostics (\cref{tab:grounding}).}
We predict the ungrounded-assertion rate to drop across cycles in
line with the hallucination trend in~\cref{tab:halluc}. The
unsupported-correct rate is a harder call: a small rise is tolerable
(the model may correctly answer from parametric memory without
pointing to an episode), but a large rise would indicate that
self-improvement is encouraging the model to bypass retrieval even
when retrieval is available and should be investigated.

\paragraph{Continual learning (\cref{tab:continual}).}
We predict the MMLU retention ratio $\rho$ to stay $\ge 0.95$ across
cycles under the EWC anchor, and never to fall below $0.90$. BWT is
predicted to be small and non-negative in expectation. FWT is
predicted to be weakly positive on the transfer-eval benchmarks. A
retention ratio $\rho < 0.90$ at any cycle would falsify the
EWC-anchor design commitment and, per the falsification plan in
\cref{sec:ablation}, would be the signal that SIL is displacing
pre-training knowledge rather than augmenting it.

\paragraph{Purity validation and trajectory
(\cref{tab:purity}, \cref{tab:purity-convergence},
\cref{fig:purity-trajectory}).}
The theorem-versus-measurement table is the sharpest test of the
theoretical chapter. We predict $|P_{\text{obs}} -
P_{\text{theory}}| \le 0.03$ at every cycle, within the 95\%
paired-seed CI, with the residual attributable to finite-sample
verifier noise. The first-difference column $\Delta P_c$ is predicted
to be non-negative at every cycle (the monotonicity
claim of~\cref{thm:monotonicity}) and to decay toward zero as the
purity envelope saturates (the convergence claim
of~\cref{thm:convergence}). A sustained $|P_{\text{obs}} -
P_{\text{theory}}| > 0.05$, or a negative $\Delta P_c$ outside the CI,
would require a direct revision of the theoretical section; per the
review discipline committed in~\cref{sec:ablation-results}, such a
finding is disclosed as the headline outcome regardless of whether
CAEM beats the baselines on accuracy.

\paragraph{Significance (\cref{tab:sig-test}).}
We predict that Holm--Bonferroni-adjusted McNemar $p < 0.05$ will
hold for \caem{} versus B1--B4 (the test-time-only baselines) on all
three training benchmarks, and for \caem{} versus B6--B7 (vanilla
fine-tuning and EWC-only fine-tuning) on at least two of the three
training benchmarks. We do \emph{not} pre-register a prediction of
significance against B5 (FLARE) and B8 (Self-RAG citation-only),
because those baselines may match or exceed CAEM on the grounding
and citation-quality dimensions respectively and the paired contrast
there is expected to be close to zero. This asymmetry is deliberate:
pre-registering a significance claim only where the mechanism of
CAEM differentiates it from the baseline prevents overclaiming on
comparisons where the baselines are designed-for-task.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:ch5-summary}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter~5 fixes the experimental protocol for the Phase~2 run: a
six-benchmark factual-QA panel with an MMLU retention control, the
CES geometric-mean composite with its five validated axes, the eight
external baselines (plus one optional STaR ceiling), and the
seventeen-variant cyclic ablation registry. The seven metric-category
tables (\cref{tab:headline,tab:calibration,tab:halluc,tab:routing,tab:verifier-attrib,tab:memory-growth,tab:ces})
and the ablation table (\cref{tab:ablation-main}) are scaffolded
above and populated by Phase~2; the three theoretical validations
(\cref{thm:purity,thm:monotonicity,thm:convergence}) are checked
against the same confirmatory-pass trace. The methodological claims
of~\cref{ch:requirements,ch:methodology} are the objects under test.

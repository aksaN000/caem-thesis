\section{Background}

Large Language Models (LLMs) have displayed exceptional proficiency across various NLP (Natural Language Processing) tasks such as answering questions, summarizing, generating codes and reasoning. Models such as GPT-4, Claude and PaLM achieve impressive performance on usual benchmarks, with in some cases, even exceeding human-level accuracy on certain tasks \cite{openai2023gpt4, anthropic2023claude, anil2023palm}. However, a critical limitation hampers their deployment in high-stakes applications, for example, the generation of plausible yet factually incorrect responses, commonly known as \textit{hallucinations}.

In LLMs, hallucination occurs when models produce outputs that contradict source material, conflict with valid world knowledge, or even fabricate information entirely. Recent studies reveal concerning statistics about hallucination occurrences. The TruthfulQA benchmark, introduced by Lin et al. \cite{lin-etal-2022-truthfulqa}, is designed to particularly measure how models copy human falsehoods. The worst part is that it found larger models performing worse than small ones. For example, GPT-3 175B achieved only 58\% on truthfulness alone and just 21--25\% on the combined truthful-and-informative metric, compared to 94\% human accuracy on both metrics, demonstrating an \textit{inverse scaling} phenomenon where increased model size does not guarantee improved truthfulness. Also, another benchmark, FActScore by Min et al. \cite{min-etal-2023-factscore}, developed for fine-grained evaluation of long-form generation, shows that ChatGPT achieved only 58\% supported atomic facts in biography generation. Domain-specific studies show even higher error rates. For example, medical literature analysis by Alkaissi and McFarlane \cite{alkaissi2023artificial} found hallucination rates of 69-77\% in ChatGPT responses to clinical questions.

The fundamental challenge expands beyond accuracy benchmarks. Current LLMs operate statelessly where each query is processed independently without learning from previous interactions or collecting verified knowledge. This stateless architecture results in three critical limitations. Firstly, \textit{no memory of successful reasoning patterns}, which requires generation from scratch for every query. Secondly, \textit{poor confidence calibration}, where models show high confidence even while being incorrect \cite{kadavath2022language}. Lastly, \textit{no self-improvement mechanism}, which causes indefinite repetition of identical errors without adapting.

All these limitations have significant practical consequences. For example, in educational sectors, students may learn incorrect information presented confidently. In healthcare sectors, hallucinated medical information can lead to harmful decisions. In enterprise knowledge management, fake citations and sources can hamper trust. The stateless nature also causes the repetition of inefficient-expensive generation for similar queries rather than using past verified reasonings.

This research addresses these fundamental limitations through CAEM (Confidence-Aware Episodic Memory with Self-Improvement), a system that targets a substantial reduction in the specific subclass of confident \textit{factuality} hallucinations that matter for knowledge-intensive question answering (confabulations in the sense of \citet{farquhar2024semantic}, ungrounded factual claims, and claims that contradict retrieved evidence), through three architectural commitments. First, a \textit{verified episodic memory} that admits a reasoning chain only after a post-generation verifier, organised into three complementary families whose weighted composite aggregates six of nine signals and is augmented by a separately-consumed contradiction veto, has passed it through a four-outcome decision (store, defer, abstain, or discard), so that stored knowledge is distinguished from marginal or uncertain output rather than collapsed into a single binary gate. Second, a \textit{confidence-aware three-tier router} that combines stored-answer quality with query-to-memory similarity and overlays an independent safety override driven by a pre-routing uncertainty estimate, so that forced grounding is available before generation has started rather than only after it has failed. Third, a \textit{constrained self-improvement loop} that fine-tunes on the high-quality training pool under an $L_2$ anchor regulariser and aborts with a weight rollback if an out-of-distribution retention probe drops below a fixed ratio, so that accumulation across cycles is compounding rather than drifting. The pre-registered quantitative target is a relative reduction of at least 40\% in the pooled confident-error rate against a zero-shot Flan-T5-Large baseline on a multi-benchmark factual-QA panel, using the diagnostic defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:requirements} and reported cycle-by-cycle in Chapter~5.

\section{Rationale of the Study or Motivation}

The motivation for this research is that there is a wide gap between what LLM are capable of and what is required when they are used. While current models are capable of writing fluent, relevant text with excellent capacity, they usually cannot distinguish between \textit{real facts} and \textit{confabulation} (the subclass of factuality hallucination in which the model is internally confident but would just as easily have generated a different answer on a resample~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}), which makes it difficult to apply them in domains requiring precision.

The existing approaches to decrease the number of hallucinations could be classified into three categories, with all of them possessing considerable limitations. Firstly, \textit{Retrieval-augmented generation} (RAG) methods \cite{NEURIPS2020_6b493230}, which looks up documents and uses them for generation. But, the model's answer is as good as the documents it retrieves and only depends on retrieval quality, not experience. Secondly, \textit{Prompting techniques} such as chain-of-thought \cite{NEURIPS2022_9d560961} and self-consistency \cite{wang2023selfconsistency} makes the model think more carefully and checks multiple reasoning paths, but cannot remember information later and each session starts with no knowledge stored. Thirdly, \textit{post-generation verification methods} \cite{manakul-etal-2023-selfcheckgpt, farquhar2024semantic}, which check the answer after it is generated and can detect hallucinations after they occur, but do not teach the model what to do next time to reduce mistakes. So the same hallucinations can happen again in the future.

The key insight driving this research is that hallucination reduction requires \textit{architectural intervention} rather than incremental improvements to existing methods. Specifically, three capabilities are essential but absent from current systems. First, models need \textit{verified episodic memory} whose storage decision is not a single binary threshold but a four-outcome cascade, so that an answer which is plausible but has not cleared the commit bar is deferred for a second pass rather than committed to Tier~1 retrieval or discarded outright. Second, models require \textit{confidence-aware routing} that reads pre-routing uncertainty as an independent safety signal alongside a combined similarity-and-stored-quality score, so that low model readiness triggers forced external grounding before generation has started rather than only after an overconfident answer has propagated through the pipeline. Third, systems must support \textit{constrained self-improvement}, in which fine-tuning on a high-quality training pool is bounded by a regulariser that pulls weights toward the base model and an out-of-distribution retention probe that aborts the cycle if general capability has eroded, so that knowledge accumulation compounds across cycles rather than drifting into the benchmark at the expense of the base capability.

This research is significant for both theoretical and practical reasons. Theoretically, it addresses the open question of whether imperfect verification can enable monotonic improvement in self-training systems. Prior work in semi-supervised learning demonstrates that noisy labels can degrade model performance \cite{natarajan2013learning}, yet our preliminary analysis suggests that when base model accuracy exceeds random chance, verified data purity can exceed verification accuracy itself through favorable base rate dynamics. This counterintuitive property, if validated empirically, provides theoretical justification for self-improvement under realistic verification constraints.

Practically, a reduction of at least 40\% in the confident-error rate of a 780M-parameter model (the pre-registered target; see Chapter~5) would make language models much more useful without hampering their overall abilities. This would allow educational systems to safely learn and remember specific institutional knowledge. Also, it would help businesses gradually build reliable domain expertise through verified interactions. In healthcare and law domains, it would preserve language understanding while maintaining strict accuracy standards. Additionally, using memory to reuse past answers would significantly lower computation costs, making AI systems more affordable and sustainable. The pre-registered target of at least a 40\% relative reduction, and the reduction actually achieved by CAEM, are reported in Chapter~5 relative to a zero-shot Flan-T5-Large baseline under the confident-error-rate diagnostic defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:requirements}.

The undergraduate thesis scope is appropriate because the main ideas can be tested using well-known models like \textit{Flan-T5-Large}, a factual-QA benchmark panel (\textit{FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, TruthfulQA, StrategyQA,} and \textit{ARC-Challenge}) with \textit{MMLU} as an out-of-distribution retention control, and manageable computational resources compatible with a single RTX 4090 (24\,GB VRAM). A ten-cycle self-improvement trajectory is used so that the memory has enough time to approach equilibrium under the retention guard, in line with the 7--10 cycle horizons typical of continual-learning equilibrium studies. A 12-month timeline is enough to properly evaluate the system while focusing only on factual and knowledge-based tasks, where correctness can be clearly checked, and excluding creative tasks that are harder to evaluate objectively.

\section{Problem Statement}

Current large language models suffer from three fundamental limitations that prevent their reliable deployment in knowledge-intensive applications which requires factual accuracy:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stateless Operation Without Knowledge Accumulation:} LLMs process each query independently without retaining knowledge from previous interactions. Even if the model thinks carefully and gets the answer right, all the thinking is discarded once the answer is generated. So, if a similar question is asked again, the model must redo all the thinking again from the beginning. This stateless architecture causes two problems. Firstly, it wastes time as well as computing power by repeating the work. Secondly, the model might give a completely different answer next time, even for the same question. The absence of episodic memory prevents it from learning any domain-specific knowledge as each interaction is separated and does not help the model to get better over time.
    
    \item \textbf{Poor Confidence Calibration and Uncertainty Quantification:} Modern neural networks are often more confident than they should be, meaning they sound sure even when they are wrong. Guo et al. \cite{pmlr-v70-guo17a} shows that even when the model is correct only 70\% of the time, it may act like it is 90\% sure. This mismatch is dangerous because people may trust wrong answers too much. This problem shows up in two main ways. Firstly, the model gives a wrong answer very confidently, so users do not realize they should double-check it. Secondly, the model sometimes gives a correct answer but may sound unsure, which causes additional checking that was not required. If the model can not judge its own confidence properly, it can not decide if a quick lookup is enough or when deeper reasoning is needed. Current single-signal confidence methods achieve only 0.50-0.60 AUROC for telling whether an answer is right or wrong \cite{xiong2023can}, barely above random chance.
    
    \item \textbf{Absence of Self-Improvement Mechanisms:} Deployed LLMs remain static after training, unable to learn from mistakes or adapt to domain-specific patterns. If a model hallucinates, it will keep making the same mistake forever unless humans retrain it again. Trying to teach the model new things through naive fine-tuning causes a big problem called \textit{catastrophic forgetting} \cite{doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114}, where learning new information causes 30-50\% degradation of already acquired capabilities. The absence of self-improvement means that verification efforts provide no long-term value, for example, verifying 1000 answers today provides no benefit tomorrow, as the system cannot combine verified knowledge into improved generation.
\end{itemize}

These three limitations make each other worse, creating systems that remain simultaneously overconfident and unreliable, expensive yet static, capable yet ultimately unsuitable for high-stakes deployment. This research addresses all three simultaneously through architectural innovation: verified episodic memory with a four-outcome storage decision for knowledge accumulation, confidence-aware three-tier routing with a pre-routing safety override for reliable dispatch, and constrained self-improvement under an $L_2$ anchor regulariser and an out-of-distribution retention guard that aborts the cycle with weight rollback if general capability has eroded.

\section{Objective}

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate CAEM (Confidence-Aware Episodic Memory with Self-Improvement), a system that addresses the three limitations identified in the Problem Statement --- stateless operation, poor confidence calibration, and the absence of self-improvement --- in a single architecture. Concretely, CAEM reduces \textit{confident factuality hallucination} (the subclass defined in §Background, comprising confabulations~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}, ungrounded factual claims, and claims that contradict retrieved evidence) in a 780M-parameter instruction-tuned language model through accumulated verified knowledge, pre-routing and post-generation uncertainty quantification, and constrained iterative fine-tuning under a retention guard. This comprehensive goal decomposes into seven specific objectives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Design a Verified Episodic Memory with a Four-Outcome Storage Decision:} Develop an episodic memory architecture that admits a question-reasoning-answer tuple only after a post-generation verifier has emitted one of four outcomes (store, defer, abstain, or discard) based on the composite verification score, an atomic-level grounding pass, and an explicit contradiction veto. Defer entries that are plausible but have not cleared the commit bar into a bounded first-in-first-out buffer with a time-to-live, so that a later cycle can reconsider them rather than discarding the signal. Implement value-score pruning combining recency, retrieval frequency, and success rate, with a novelty filter that suppresses near-duplicate writes.

    \item \textbf{Develop Pre-Routing Confidence Estimation Independent of Generation:} Implement an encoder-only confidence estimate formed by fusing a token-probability aggregate over a short greedy prefix and an encoder convergence ratio that measures how much the late encoder layers disagree with the early layers on the same input. Temperature-scale the fused scalar against a held-out calibration split so that reported confidence matches empirical accuracy. The estimate is paid per query regardless of tier and is used independently of the stored-answer quality score to drive the safety override in the router.

    \item \textbf{Implement Three-Tier Adaptive Routing with an Independent Safety Override:} Design a routing rule that combines query-to-memory similarity with the stored-answer quality of the nearest neighbour into a weighted combined score and dispatches to Tier~1 (retrieve), Tier~2 (zero-shot generate), or Tier~3 (retrieval-augmented generate) based on three thresholds. Overlay an independent safety override: if the pre-routing confidence falls below a fixed floor, force Tier~3 regardless of the combined score, so that forced grounding is available before generation has started and is not entangled with the quality of memory the query happens to land near.

    \item \textbf{Implement a Nine-Signal Post-Generation Verifier Organised into Three Complementary Families:} Combine three internal-calibration signals (token-probability aggregate on the generated answer, MC-dropout plurality, and a fused internal scalar), three sample-set signals (average pairwise similarity across sampled chains, chain-to-answer entailment, and normalised semantic entropy following Farquhar et~al.~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}), and three external-grounding signals (max-match, mean-match, and weakest-link atomic-fact entailment against retrieved evidence) into a nine-signal record whose weighted composite $\ustored$ aggregates six of the nine. A separately-consumed ensemble-max contradiction probability acts as a hard veto on the storage decision rather than as a weighted composite input, so that any output that retrieval evidence actively refutes is rejected regardless of how well it scored on the composite. Gate the composite additionally with a confabulation early-exit rule that fires when a high internal-calibration signal coincides with a low max-match grounding score, so that internally-confident but externally-unsupported outputs are caught before storage.

    \item \textbf{Implement Retroactive Re-Verification and Deferred-Entry Reconsideration as Cycle-Boundary Memory Update Passes:} Because three of the nine verifier signals read the model's internal state directly, stored composites are model-relative snapshots that drift with every fine-tune. At each cycle boundary, re-score every stored episode under the updated model using an upward-only update rule that promotes entries whose composite has improved past a re-verification threshold and categorically prunes those that have fallen below it. Run a companion pass over the deferred buffer that promotes entries now above the store threshold, drops those that exceed the time-to-live, and requeues the rest. Both passes are abort-guarded and expose per-cycle counter diagnostics.

    \item \textbf{Implement Constrained Self-Improvement with a Retention Guard and Weight Rollback:} Curate the training pool from entries that pass a strict quality filter (a training threshold set above the store threshold) so that training-pool quality is held to a higher bar than Tier~1 retrieval quality. Fine-tune on a 90/10 mix of verified reasoning chains and general-QA pairs under an $L_2$ anchor regulariser toward the base-model parameters as an EWC approximation~\cite{doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114}. Probe MMLU before and after fine-tuning in each cycle and abort the cycle with a weight rollback if the retention ratio falls below a fixed floor; the memory is never rolled back, so knowledge accumulation survives abort events even when weights do not.

    \item \textbf{Evaluate on a Factual-QA Panel with an Out-of-Distribution Retention Control and a Geometric Composite Score:} Evaluate on FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, and ARC-Challenge as the primary factual-QA panel, with MMLU as the out-of-distribution retention control. Report a Composite Evaluation Score defined as the geometric mean of five axes (answer accuracy, epistemic quality, retention, calibration, and verification reliability) and a confident-error-rate diagnostic measuring the fraction of responses that are both high-confidence under the stored-quality score and incorrect under the benchmark's correctness metric. Compare against zero-shot Flan-T5-Large, a RAG-only baseline, and per-mechanism ablations of the verifier composite, the router overlay, the retroactive re-verification pass, and the $L_2$ anchor.
\end{itemize}

These objectives collectively address the three fundamental limitations identified in the Problem Statement. Objectives one through four provide the per-query mechanism: verified episodic memory with a four-outcome storage gate, pre-routing confidence, three-tier routing with a safety override, and a nine-signal post-generation verifier. Objectives five and six provide the cycle-boundary mechanism: retroactive re-verification, deferred reconsideration, and constrained self-improvement under a retention guard. Objective seven provides the evaluation framework that tests whether the joint system improves along the axes the design optimises for while preserving the out-of-distribution capability the guard protects.

\section{Methodology in Brief}

This section provides a high-level overview of the CAEM system architecture. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} develops each stage in full, with equations, algorithms, and hyperparameters.

\subsection{System Architecture Overview}

CAEM organises a single query into an eight-stage pipeline that is traversed once per query and wrapped in a per-cycle update loop. Stage~1 produces a \textit{pre-routing confidence} $\upre$ from an encoder-only pass, fusing a short-prefix token-probability aggregate with an encoder convergence ratio. Stage~2 encodes the query with Sentence-BERT and searches a FAISS-indexed episodic memory for the nearest stored entry, returning a similarity score $s$ and the neighbour's stored-quality score $\ustored$. Stage~3 dispatches the query to one of three tiers using the combined routing score $\mathcal{S} = 0.70\,s + 0.30\,\ustored$, overlaid with an independent safety override that forces Tier~3 whenever $\upre$ falls below a fixed floor. Stage~4 executes the tier: Tier~1 serves the neighbour's stored answer directly; Tier~2 runs a zero-shot generation on the fine-tuned model; Tier~3 retrieves Wikipedia evidence and runs a retrieval-augmented generation. Stage~5 passes the tier output through a post-generation verifier whose nine signals are organised into three families (internal calibration, sample-set agreement, external grounding); a weighted composite $\ustored$ aggregates six of the nine, and a separately-consumed ensemble-max contradiction probability acts as a hard veto in Stage~6 rather than as a weighted input to $\ustored$. Stage~6 emits one of four outcomes (store, defer, abstain, or discard) from the composite, an ensemble-max contradiction probability, and a max-match grounding score; Stage~7 executes the implied memory and user-facing actions. Stage~8 runs at cycle boundaries: a retroactive re-verification pass refreshes stored composites under the updated model, a deferred-entry reconsideration pass sweeps the deferred buffer, a fine-tuning step accumulates weights under an $L_2$ anchor, and an MMLU retention probe aborts the cycle with a weight rollback if general capability has dropped below a fixed floor. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} gives the high-level view; the full per-stage pipeline appears as Figure~4.1 in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\subsection{Query Processing and Routing}

Every incoming query is first encoded by Sentence-BERT as a 768-dimensional, $L_2$-normalised embedding, then looked up in a FAISS index. The lookup returns the similarity $s$ to the nearest stored entry and that entry's stored-quality score $\ustored$. In parallel, Stage~1 produces the pre-routing confidence $\upre$ from an encoder-only pass that fuses a token-probability aggregate with the encoder convergence ratio and is temperature-scaled against a held-out calibration split. The router combines $s$ and $\ustored$ into a single routing score $\mathcal{S} = 0.70\,s + 0.30\,\ustored$ and dispatches the query to Tier~1 (direct retrieval) when the combined score is high enough that the stored answer can be served without regeneration, to Tier~2 (zero-shot generation on the fine-tuned model) in the middle band, and to Tier~3 (retrieval-augmented generation against Wikipedia) when the combined score is low. An independent safety override is layered on top of this dispatch: if $\upre$ falls below a fixed floor, the query is forced to Tier~3 regardless of the combined score, so that low model readiness triggers forced external grounding before generation has started rather than only after an overconfident answer has been produced. This override is the two-mechanism separation that distinguishes CAEM's router from a pure similarity-threshold dispatcher. As memory coverage grows across cycles, a greater fraction of queries land on Tier~1, yielding efficiency gains without routing queries that the model is not ready for to the cheapest path.

\subsection{Post-Generation Verification and the Four-Outcome Storage Decision}

All tier outputs undergo a nine-signal post-generation verifier organised into three complementary families, so that no single exploitable signal can drive storage on its own. The \textit{internal calibration} family (token-probability aggregate on the generated answer, MC-dropout plurality~\cite{pmlr-v48-gal16}, and a fused internal scalar) measures what the model itself thinks of its output. The \textit{sample-set} family (average pairwise similarity of sampled chains, chain-to-answer entailment, and normalised semantic entropy~\cite{farquhar2024semantic}) measures the stability of the answer across stochastic decodes. The \textit{external grounding} family (max-match and mean-match entailment against retrieved evidence) measures whether retrieved evidence supports the claim, augmented by a weakest-link atomic-fact decomposition that catches partially-unsupported answers. The nine signals are combined into a composite score $\ustored$. A confabulation early-exit rule short-circuits storage whenever semantic entropy is high; a contradiction veto rejects any output whose grounding pass returns a high ensemble-max contradiction. The surviving composite feeds a four-outcome storage decision: \textsc{Store} if the composite is above the store threshold, \textsc{Deferred} if it falls in a lower band that is plausible but has not cleared the commit bar, \textsc{Abstain} if the composite is low and grounding is also low (an explicit refusal rather than a silent failure), and \textsc{Discard} otherwise. Deferred entries enter a bounded first-in-first-out buffer with a time-to-live and are reconsidered at the next cycle boundary by a dedicated pass that parallels the retroactive re-verification of committed memory.

\subsection{Constrained Self-Improvement Loop}

CAEM implements iterative refinement over ten cycles. Each cycle processes a workload of questions through the current system, collecting verified episodes, deferred entries, and contradiction-veto or abstain cases. At the cycle boundary, a retroactive re-verification pass re-scores every stored episode under the updated model with an upward-only rule (promote when the composite has improved past a re-verification threshold, prune when it has fallen below); a deferred-entry reconsideration pass promotes, drops by time-to-live, or requeues each held entry. Fine-tuning then draws a training pool from entries that pass a strict quality filter (a training threshold set above the store threshold, so that training-pool quality is held to a higher bar than Tier~1 retrieval quality), and optimises a mixed batch (90\% verified reasoning chains, 10\% general-QA) under an $L_2$ anchor regulariser toward the base model as an EWC approximation~\cite{doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114}. Before and after the fine-tune, the cycle probes MMLU~\cite{hendrycks2021measuring} and computes a retention ratio. If the retention ratio falls below a fixed floor, the cycle aborts: weights are rolled back to the previous checkpoint and retroverify is skipped for that cycle. The memory, however, is never rolled back, so verified knowledge accumulated during the aborted cycle survives even when the weights do not. This asymmetry decouples weight-level drift from memory-level accumulation and is what makes the self-improvement loop safe to iterate.

\subsection{Implementation}

The system employs Flan-T5-Large (780M parameters)~\cite{JMLR:v25:23-0870} as the only trainable component for its instruction-tuned capabilities and encoder-decoder architecture. Sentence-BERT (all-mpnet-base-v2, 110M, frozen)~\cite{reimers-gurevych-2019-sentence} converts queries into 768-dimensional embeddings; FAISS~\cite{8733051} backs the episodic memory; MiniCheck-Flan-T5-Large (770M, frozen)~\cite{tang2024minicheck}, trained on language-model-generated claim-support data, performs the claim-verification checks that feed the grounding and entailment signals. The legacy generic-NLI backend (RoBERTa-Large-MNLI~\cite{liu2019roberta}) is retained as an ablation target (Chapter 5 variant \texttt{roberta\_nli\_backend}) so the backend choice is defended by trajectory evidence rather than citation alone. All components fit in the 24\,GB VRAM of a single RTX 4090 under bfloat16 mixed precision, with fine-tuning made memory-feasible by the $L_2$ anchor (which requires only the base-model parameters on-device, rather than a full Fisher Information Matrix). Evaluation uses FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, and ARC-Challenge as the primary factual-QA panel, with MMLU as an out-of-distribution retention control, reporting the geometric Composite Evaluation Score (Chapter~\ref{ch:requirements}) and a confident-error-rate diagnostic. The 12-month timeline allocates four months for implementation, four for experimental cycles, and four for analysis and writing.

\section{Scopes and Challenges}

\subsection{Research Scope}

This research focuses on tasks where there is a clear right or wrong answer and not subjective questions. The system's target is to handle questions that need to find information from sources, connect multiple pieces of information, verify facts, and avoid common false beliefs. The six primary evaluation benchmarks (FEVER, TriviaQA, Natural Questions, TruthfulQA, StrategyQA, and ARC-Challenge) are chosen because they have questions with definite answers that can be checked objectively under per-benchmark correctness metrics (exact match, token-F1, label accuracy, or ROUGE-L depending on the task family). MMLU is reserved as an out-of-distribution retention control rather than a target benchmark: the fine-tune is not optimised for MMLU, but the retention guard aborts any cycle in which MMLU accuracy drops below a fixed fraction of the pre-cycle baseline.

The scope explicitly \textit{excludes} creative generation tasks such as story writing, poetry composition, or open-ended dialogue where evaluation criteria are subjective and multiple valid responses exist. Similarly, tasks requiring up-to-date real-world information beyond the training cutoff such as stock prices, breaking news and current events are outside scope, as the system lacks mechanisms for continuous knowledge based updates. Important grounds like Code generation and mathematical theorem proving are excluded to maintain focus on natural language reasoning. The system operates on English text only, with the scope of working on multilingual capabilities in the future.

The model selection reflects resource constraints appropriate for undergraduate thesis work. Flan-T5-Large (780M parameters) provides sufficient capacity for complex reasoning while remaining feasible for fine-tuning with university GPU allocations. Larger models such as LLaMA-13B or GPT-3.5 scale would require computational resources that are too expensive and resource-heavy. The ten-cycle experimental design is sized to capture the essential self-improvement dynamics and allow the memory to approach equilibrium under the retention guard, while remaining achievable on a single RTX 4090 within a 12-month timeline.

Per-benchmark evaluation uses a fixed sub-sample sized for meaningful statistical power without excessive computational cost. The episodic memory is bounded at a target capacity of $10^6$ entries with a flat FAISS index promoted to IVF-PQ above a threshold; for the ten-cycle experiments reported in Chapter~5 the memory is initialised empty and grows up to the low tens of thousands of entries, which is well within single-GPU capacity and exercises the novelty filter and value-score pruning policies without saturating them.

\subsection{Technical Challenges}

The implementation of CAEM faces six primary technical challenges that must be addressed:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Verifier Reliability and Stored-Quality Drift:} The nine-signal post-generation verifier produces a composite score $\ustored$ that is used as both a Tier-1 retrieval proxy and a training-pool quality filter. Three of the nine signals (the internal-calibration family) read the model's internal state directly and drift with every fine-tune, so stored composites are model-relative snapshots that must be refreshed at each cycle boundary. The retroactive re-verification pass addresses this drift with an upward-only update rule: only entries whose composite has improved past a re-verification threshold are promoted, while those that have fallen below it are categorically pruned. The cost is the full verifier re-scoring of every stored episode at each cycle boundary.

    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Forgetting Under Iterative Fine-Tuning:} Fine-tuning on domain-specific verified examples risks degrading general language capabilities. CAEM addresses this through three layers of defence: a strict training-pool quality filter at a threshold above the store threshold, a 90/10 mix of verified reasoning chains and general-QA pairs in each training batch, and an $L_2$ anchor toward the base-model parameters that approximates EWC under a uniform-Fisher assumption~\cite{doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114}. A hard out-of-distribution retention guard on MMLU aborts the cycle and rolls back weights if the retention ratio falls below a fixed floor; memory is never rolled back, so the fine-tune failing in cycle $k$ does not lose the verified episodes accumulated during it. The effectiveness of each layer is validated empirically through the ablation grid in Chapter~\ref{ch:requirements}.

    \item \textbf{Pre-Routing Confidence Calibration:} The pre-routing confidence $\upre$ is used both as a one-dimensional scalar in the combined routing score and as the sole input to the safety override, so its calibration matters directly. CAEM fuses two signals (a short-prefix token-probability aggregate and an encoder convergence ratio) and temperature-scales the fused scalar against a held-out calibration split by L-BFGS on binary negative-log-likelihood. The non-obvious design choice is that confidence is computed from the encoder alone (no decode) so that its cost is paid per query regardless of tier; the cost of the signals it avoids (MC-dropout plurality, sample-set agreement, semantic entropy) is deferred to the post-generation verifier on Tier~2 and Tier~3 paths only.

    \item \textbf{Memory Retrieval Quality and Novelty Filtering:} The system uses FAISS for approximate nearest-neighbour search, which introduces the possibility of missing a semantically-similar entry if the embedding happens to fall near an index partition boundary. Near-duplicates are filtered at write time by a novelty threshold on cosine similarity (suppressing new writes when a stored neighbour exceeds the threshold); pruning at capacity uses a value score that combines retrieval frequency, success rate, recency, and storage-time confidence. The novelty threshold is a tuning knob: too high admits many near-duplicates that bloat the index without adding coverage, too low rejects paraphrase-level rewording that would have been genuinely useful additions.

    \item \textbf{Baseline Performance Dependency for Self-Improvement:} The self-improvement argument depends on the base model performing above random chance on the target benchmarks, because training on verified-but-imperfect episodes can only compound accuracy when the verifier's false-positive rate is low enough that the training pool is cleaner than the raw model's outputs. To reduce this risk, the project chooses Flan-T5-Large, which already performs competitively on factual-QA after instruction tuning, and picks benchmarks where the base model clears the threshold. Benchmarks on which the base model underperforms (e.g., narrow-domain subsets where zero-shot accuracy is near chance) are excluded from the training pool and used only for evaluation.

    \item \textbf{Evaluation Metric Selection:} Measuring hallucination reduction requires careful metric design beyond simple accuracy. CAEM's primary metric is the confident-error rate: the fraction of responses that are both high-confidence under $\ustored$ and incorrect under the benchmark's correctness metric. This captures the central clinical concern (confidently-wrong outputs) more directly than overall accuracy. A geometric Composite Evaluation Score (accuracy, epistemic quality, retention, calibration, and verification reliability) provides the joint-axis view; the geometric mean penalises any axis that collapses, which is the behaviour that matches the use-case (a system that sacrifices retention for accuracy is not acceptable). Comparison against strong baselines (zero-shot Flan-T5-Large, a RAG-only variant, and per-mechanism ablations) is run under identical benchmarks and metric definitions to avoid the confound of re-implementing published baselines with different evaluation choices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mitigation Strategies}

For each challenge identified, the research design incorporates mitigation strategies. Verifier-reliability and stored-quality drift are addressed by the retroactive re-verification pass, which re-scores every stored episode at each cycle boundary under the updated model and applies an upward-only update rule. Catastrophic forgetting is addressed by the combination of the strict training-pool quality filter ($\tau_{\text{train}} > \tau_{\text{store}}$), the 90/10 episode-general batch mix, the $L_2$ anchor regulariser, and the hard MMLU retention guard that aborts the cycle with a weight rollback if the retention ratio falls below the fixed floor $\rho_{\min} = 0.93$. Pre-routing confidence calibration is tackled by temperature-scaling the fused scalar on a held-out calibration split before deployment, rather than on-the-fly adaptation. Memory retrieval quality is managed by the novelty filter at write time (suppressing near-duplicate writes at a cosine similarity threshold of 0.95) and by value-score pruning that triggers only when capacity is exceeded. Baseline-performance dependency is managed by benchmark selection and by the verifier's contradiction veto and confabulation early-exit rule, both of which reduce the expected false-positive rate of the storage decision below the raw composite-threshold baseline. Evaluation metric risk is managed by reporting both the geometric Composite Evaluation Score and the confident-error-rate diagnostic, so that no single collapsing axis can mask a regression on another.

Although the problems are significant, they can be managed within the resources and the time frame. The aim is to improve the system clearly and measurably while making the architectural commitments that the current state of the literature supports.
